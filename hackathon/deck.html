02 · The game

Sixteen taps.
Then the app knows your taste.

Pick a theme: "Best world cuisine", "Your 2020s music", "Brands that match your style", "Movies you'd want at your funeral". The game pairs items in a bracket. A or B, tap the one that is closer to you. Sixteen fights, about four minutes, and you get your top-3 plus a taste type with a four-paragraph editorial write-up.

Every theme you play adds a layer to your profile. After enough themes the app writes you an AI portrait that weaves the whole profile together. Friends play the same theme and see how close their taste is to yours, with a per-category fit score. Every result has a share card.

03 · Where we start from

What is live today,
and what the Android app really is.

In production since June 2026

  • The game on web and Google Play, RU and EN
  • AI portraits written by Claude from the user's real results, with per-portrait share cards and public pages
  • Friends: requests, result comparison, fit score per category
  • Daily theme with push notifications, comeback and streak pushes
  • Achievements, visit streaks, user-suggested themes with a moderation queue
  • Backend: FastAPI + SQLite, admin panel, live event stream, backups

The Android app, honestly

A Capacitor shell that loads the web app from our server, with native push (FCM), haptics, the share sheet and Google sign-in. It works, it is on Google Play, and it is the kind of wrapper this hackathon is right not to reward. No wallet, no on-chain interaction, no SKR anywhere. That is the starting line, and the commit history in the repository shows it.

04 · Why this belongs on Seeker

A daily four-minute habit
where the wallet is the account.

Habit

Daily theme, streaks, friends

One theme a day, a push when it is out, a streak to protect, a friend to compare with. The loop already runs in production. On Seeker the same loop runs with the wallet as identity, so there is no sign-up form between a new user and the first tournament.

Something worth paying for

Portraits cost real money

Every AI portrait is a paid API call on our side. Today we give them away. SKR becomes the way to pay for the one thing in the app that has a real cost, which makes the SKR integration a business mechanic, not decoration.

Seeker-only

Genesis Token perks

Seeker owners get their first portrait free, a Seeker achievement and an exclusive tournament. The Genesis Token mint address is the anti-sybil key, so the perks cannot be farmed from throwaway wallets.

Share loop

Cards leave the app

Every result and every portrait has a share card and a public page. On Seeker that means every shared card is an install path into the dApp Store listing.

05 · What we build during Clock In

Five things,
in this order.

Tags show the state of each item and are updated as work ships. Everything below is scoped to land before the October 8 deadline; whatever does not land is removed from this deck before submission.

1

Wallet sign-in through Mobile Wallet Adapter planned
A native Kotlin bridge inside the Capacitor app, using the official Android MWA client library, exposed to the web client as a small plugin (authorize, sign message, sign and send). Works with Seed Vault, Phantom and Solflare. The app never sees a key.

2

AI portraits paid in SKR planned
Tap "get my portrait", the wallet is asked to send a small amount of SKR to the Tappetite treasury, the backend verifies the transfer on-chain and only then generates the portrait. Details on the next slide.

3

Seeker Genesis Token perks planned
Detect the Genesis Token in the connected wallet, record its mint address once, unlock the free first portrait, the Seeker achievement and the exclusive tournament.

4

A real APK, not a shell planned
Web assets bundled into the APK so the app starts offline, a new signing key separate from Google Play, dApp Store listing prepared.

5

Mobile polish planned
Haptic feedback on every pick, the result card through the native share sheet, wallet state in the profile.

06
06 · SKR integration

SKR pays for the portrait.

1

The user finishes a tournament and taps "Get my portrait". The backend creates an order with an id and a price in SKR.

2

The app asks the wallet through Mobile Wallet Adapter to sign and send an SPL transfer of the SKR mint to the Tappetite treasury, with the order id in the memo.

3

The backend fetches the confirmed transaction by signature and checks mint, amount, recipient and memo, and that the signature has not been used before.

4

Only then the portrait is generated and attached to the user. The transaction signature is stored next to the portrait.

5

Genesis Token holders skip the payment for their first portrait. The mint address of the token is recorded so one Seeker unlocks one free portrait.

Why this and not a badge

The cost is real

Portraits are generated with Claude and each one costs us money. Charging for them in SKR ties the ecosystem token to the one thing in the product that actually has a price. No custody, no cards, no store billing in the flow.

Price

Small, close to cost

A small amount of SKR per portrait, roughly generation cost plus a margin, set at launch from the SKR price at the time. Cooldown and free tiers stay as they are today for people without a wallet.

State

Designed, being built planned

Nothing on this slide exists yet as code. It is the first thing built after the wallet bridge, and the tag changes when it is live in the APK.

07 · Architecture

What runs where.

Android app

Capacitor + Kotlin

  • Web client bundled in the APK
  • Native plugins: push (FCM), haptics, share, preferences
  • New: MWA bridge plugin in Kotlin on the official client library
  • Release signed with a dApp Store key
Backend

FastAPI + SQLite

  • Accounts, results, friends, achievements, push batches
  • Portrait generation with Claude, spend ledger
  • New: SKR orders, on-chain verification of transfers, Genesis Token registry
  • nginx behind Cloudflare, systemd timers
Solana

Mainnet

  • Mobile Wallet Adapter, local association on the device
  • SPL transfers of the SKR mint to a treasury wallet
  • RPC reads to verify transactions and detect the Seeker Genesis Token
  • Testing on devnet in the emulator, then real devices

The client is a single-file web app, the backend is one Python service, the content library is one JSON file. Small enough for one person to move fast and for a judge to read in an evening.
